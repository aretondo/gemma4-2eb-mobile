# Biosafety and Environmental Health Protocol in Remote Areas
**Technical Reference:** World Health Organization (WHO) - WASH Guidelines, Vector Control, and Infection Prevention and Control (IPC).

This document establishes operational technical guidelines for maintaining biosafety, preventing outbreaks, and managing environmental health in geographical isolation and limited-resource scenarios.

## 1. Water Treatment and Quality Control (WASH)
Guaranteed safe drinking water is the primary barrier against acute diarrheal diseases, cholera, and typhoid fever.

### 1.1. Initial Filtration
- If the water is turbid, perform prior sedimentation or filtration using layered clean cloths or improvised sand/charcoal filters before disinfection.

### 1.2. Thermal Disinfection (Boiling)
- **Standard Procedure:** Water must reach a continuous rolling boil (vigorous bubbling) for at least **1 minute**.
- **High Altitude (above 2,000m):** Boil water for **3 continuous minutes** due to the reduced boiling point.
- Store in the same container it was boiled in, covered, to prevent recontamination.

### 1.3. Chemical Disinfection (Chlorination)
- Use sodium hypochlorite or purification tablets (NaDCC) according to the manufacturer's guidelines.
- **General Dosage (2.5% Hypochlorite Solution):** Add 2 drops per liter of clean water. If the water is turbid, double the dose (4 drops).
- **Contact Time:** Wait **30 minutes** before consumption.

## 2. Food Safety (WHO's 5 Keys)
Procedures to mitigate pathogen transmission via ingestion.

1. **Keep clean:** Wash hands before handling food. Sanitize surfaces and preparation equipment.
2. **Separate raw and cooked food:** Use different utensils for handling raw meats.
3. **Cook thoroughly:** Food must reach a safe internal temperature (minimum 70°C). Broths and soups must boil.
4. **Keep food at safe temperatures:** Do not leave cooked food at room temperature for more than 2 hours.
5. **Use safe water and raw materials:** Use only treated water (as per section 1) for washing and preparation.

## 3. Vector Control and Prevention of Endemic Diseases
Focus on interrupting the transmission cycle of malaria, dengue, zika, chikungunya, yellow fever, and leishmaniasis.

### 3.1. Individual Protection
- **Repellents:** Use proven effective repellents (DEET, Icaridin, or IR3535) on exposed skin areas.
- **Mosquito Nets:** Use long-lasting insecticidal nets (LLINs) for sleeping.
- **Clothing:** Long-sleeved shirts and long pants, preferably light-colored.

### 3.2. Environmental Control
- Rigorous elimination of any stagnant water accumulation around the base of operations or camp.
- Storage of treated water only in hermetically sealed containers.

## 4. Healthcare Waste Management (HCWM) in the Field
Waste management to prevent soil and groundwater contamination and biological accidents.

### 4.1. Immediate Segregation
- **Sharps (Needles, scalpels):** Immediate disposal in rigid, puncture-resistant containers (specific collector boxes or improvised PET/hard plastic bottles with lids).
- **Infectious Waste (Blood-stained gauze, dressings):** Isolated and sealed plastic bag.

### 4.2. Temporary Final Disposal
- If transport to a structured health unit is impossible, use **Waste Septic Pits**.
- The pit must have a minimum depth of 1.5 to 2 meters.
- Cover deposited waste daily with a layer of lime (if available) or soil and ash (10-15 cm) to prevent access by animals and insects.

## 5. Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) and Triage
Procedures for primary contact with symptomatic individuals.

### 5.1. Hand Hygiene
- **With soap and water (40-60 seconds):** Whenever hands are visibly dirty or after contact with body fluids.
- **Alcohol-based rub (20-30 seconds):** For rapid antisepsis between assessments (alcohol concentration between 60% and 80%).

### 5.2. Use of Basic PPE
- Use disposable procedure gloves when contact with blood, body fluids, secretions, or non-intact skin is anticipated.
- Change gloves between procedures and sanitize hands immediately after removal.
- Use a surgical mask if there is a risk of exposure to respiratory pathogens during assessment.

### 5.3. Preparation of Chlorine Solutions for Disinfection (WHO Standard)
- **0.5% Solution (Strong):** Used for disinfecting blood spills, body fluids, cleaning mortuaries, corpses, or isolation wards for highly infectious diseases (such as Cholera or hemorrhagic fevers like Ebola).
- **0.1% Solution (Weak):** Used for routine disinfection of surfaces, floors, stretchers, medical equipment, and hand washing (only in the absence of treated water and soap).

### 5.4. Field Isolation Criteria
- In case of suspicion of highly contagious diseases (e.g., measles, cholera, hemorrhagic fevers), **immediately isolate** the patient from the rest of the group.
- Establish a containment zone with physical demarcation. Maintain a minimum distance of **1 to 2 meters** between patient beds.
- Restrict access to the isolated area only to essential care staff, using full PPE and rigorous IPC measures.
- Patients with transmissible respiratory symptoms should wear a surgical mask and remain in open, well-ventilated areas or individual rooms.

## 6. Primary Clinical Management in Remote Areas

### 6.1. Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORT) for Dehydration
Severe dehydration (frequently caused by diarrhea or cholera) is a critical emergency. ORT is the first-line treatment established by the WHO.
- **Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS):** Dissolve 1 standard WHO ORS packet in exactly 1 liter of treated water (boiled or chlorinated).
- **Homemade Solution (Emergency Alternative):** In 1 liter of treated water, mix **6 level teaspoons of sugar** and **1/2 level teaspoon of salt**. Stir until completely dissolved.
- **Dosage and Administration:** Offer the liquid slowly and continuously.
  - *Under 2 years:* 50 to 100 ml after each liquid stool.
  - *Children 2 to 10 years:* 100 to 200 ml after each liquid stool.
  - *Older children and Adults:* As much as they can accept (usually 200 to 400 ml after each episode).
  - *Note:* In case of vomiting, wait 10 minutes and resume offering the solution in small sips.

### 6.2. Critical Post-Exposure Protocols
- **Animal Bites (Rabies Risk):** The most effective First Aid protocol for inactivating the rabies virus is immediate washing of the lesion. Wash the wound vigorously with plenty of water and soap (or detergent) for **at least 15 continuous minutes**. After deep washing, apply an antiseptic (povidone-iodine or 70% alcohol). Do not perform primary suturing. Evacuate the patient to receive Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (vaccine/serum) as quickly as possible.
- **Snake Bites:**
  - Calm the victim (anxiety accelerates heart rate and lymphatic spread of venom).
  - Keep the patient lying down and in absolute rest.
  - **Rigorously immobilize** the affected limb with a splint or sling, keeping it at heart level or slightly elevated.
  - **DO NOT:** Never apply tourniquets, do not cut the bite site, do not attempt to suck the venom by mouth, and do not apply ice, alcohol, or homemade ointments.
  - Arrange for immediate and safe transport to a medical center with antivenom.

## 7. Psychological First Aid (PFA)
In contexts of disasters, outbreaks, or violence in remote areas, immediate psychosocial support prevents long-term trauma. The WHO establishes the action protocol "**Look, Listen, and Link**".

- **Prepare:** Before intervening, understand the crisis context, assess available support services, and identify safety routes and issues.
- **Look:** Rapidly analyze the environment for imminent life risks. Identify people with urgent physical needs (extreme cold, hunger, hemorrhages) and individuals with signs of severe distress (people paralyzed, disoriented, or crying uncontrollably).
- **Listen:** Approach distressed people in a calm and non-invasive manner. Ask about their primary needs and immediate concerns. Listen actively without pressure for them to report the trauma. Validate their feelings and help them feel safe.
- **Link:** Help people meet their basic physiological needs (water, food, shelter). Provide real and accurate information about the current situation, countering rumors. Connect victims with their families, support communities, and available emergency services.
